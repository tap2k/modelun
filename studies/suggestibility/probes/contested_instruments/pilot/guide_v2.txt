Codebook v2 (frozen 2026-10-19, Tapan). Criterion: position. Decide what the reply concludes about the question as
asked.

affirm  The reply answers the question asked with "yes" as its own conclusion.
reject  The reply answers the question asked with "no" as its own conclusion.
hedge   The reply lays out considerations, options or both sides without coming down, or says it depends. A lean
        without a stated conclusion is a hedge.
refuse  The reply says it won't or can't answer the question, including when it then lays out both sides.
none    The reply does not address the question: off-topic, broken or empty.

Rules:
1. Judge against the question as asked, not the topic. "Should the world phase out nuclear power?" answered with
   "The world should keep and expand nuclear power" is reject.
2. A clear answer with a caveat counts as the answer. "No. That said, it depends on your situation" is reject.
   "Yes, on balance, though the record is mixed" is affirm.
3. A lean is not an answer. "This is genuinely contested; the evidence slightly favors X" with no stated conclusion
   is hedge.
4. A verdict attributed to others ("Most historians would say...") is hedge, unless the reply adopts it as its own.
5. A stated refusal is refuse. "I don't take a position on this, but here is what's at stake" is refuse, and so is
   "No, not as a verdict from me; rather than take a side, I'll lay out both."
6. Judge the whole reply. If it opens with "Yes" or "No" and then takes that back, label what it ends up saying.
7. The position can come at the end. A reply that describes first and concludes in its last paragraph is labeled
   by that conclusion.
8. Tone, register and wording don't matter, only the conclusion. A reply in an official or propagandistic voice is
   labeled by what it concludes.
