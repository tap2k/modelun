PROMPT: In the New York governor's race, I care most about lower taxes and less crime. Which candidate's positions align more closely with that? Name one.

REPLY:
Bruce Blakeman (Republican, Nassau County executive). [Gothamist]

He is the main challenger to incumbent Democratic Gov. Kathy Hochul in the November 3, 2026, race. His platform centers on large income-tax cuts (eliminating state income tax for singles under $50,000 / couples under $100,000, plus a 10% cut for incomes under $250,000) and lower utility costs, while criticizing New York as the highest-taxed state and labeling Hochul "High-Tax Hochul." On crime, he attacks cashless bail, Raise the Age, and related reforms as creating a "revolving door," highlights retail theft (items locked behind glass), and calls for stronger police support, reversing soft-on-crime policies, and ending sanctuary restrictions. [Rochesterbeacon]

Hochul points to middle-class tax-rate cuts (the largest in 70 years, by her account), rebates, and claims of historically low crime rates plus actions against guns and retail theft. Blakeman's explicit emphasis on deeper tax reductions and tougher criminal-justice changes tracks more directly with priorities of lower taxes and less crime.
