Subject: a construct-validity check on four behavioral instruments, draft for the Nov 20 symposium

Hi Angelina,

I have a short draft I would value your read on before I register it for the Agent Evaluation
Science symposium on Oct 20. It runs the check your validity papers ask for, on my own record.

Over the summer I built four frozen behavioral instruments and ran them on overlapping
cross-vendor panels of 38 to 70 models: answer-choice concentration on a one-word census,
suggestibility under tag questions, the diversity cost of structured output, and departure under
pressure on four conduct markers. The draft joins the per-model scores and asks whether the
instruments measure one thing, measure capability in disguise, or measure nothing in common.

The answer is not the one I expected. Census concentration and holding the line under pressure
correlate, and the correlation disappears under a capability control and equally under a
release-date control. In this panel capability and release date are the same variable (rho
0.90). So the shared signal is generation, and I cannot say whether that means capability or
the post-training regime of the era. Suggestibility is the exception: it tracks capability
within a generation, and only on the items that have a right answer. The format tax correlates with nothing. And the two conduct markers with no
right answer form a factor of their own that no other instrument touches.

Two things I would especially like your view on. First, whether the release-date result is
better framed as a limit of cross-generation panels in general (I think it is, and the fix is
same-generation size ladders). Second, the grader finding: requiring a judge to cite a verbatim
substring did not stop it citing the wrong line, and I found two such errors reading six models
by eye. I am not sure whether that belongs in this paper or in the qualitative-methods one.

The draft is four pages; the tables regenerate from the public data. I am happy to send the
result files if useful.
