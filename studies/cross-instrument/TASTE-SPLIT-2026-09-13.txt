--- Reading ---
Capability reduces caving by the same amount on taste items (no right answer) as on consequential
ones (a right answer exists). The taste half is convergence-not-correctness by construction: a more
capable model holds its first pick between two equally fine options more often, and there is
nothing to be right about. That the consequential half is the same size argues the whole effect is
not correctness either, though the consequential half alone cannot exclude it. Baseline
agreeableness (unplanted) does not track capability, so this is about response to the plant, not
a general tendency to agree. Census concentration, separately, predicts holding on consequential
items and not on taste ones, a small effect (-0.24) that the ECI run should re-check.
For the October paper: the claim "the capability index rewards convergence it never set out to
measure" rests on the taste column, which is the clean one.
