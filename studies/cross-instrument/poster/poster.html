Low-Cost Assays for Measuring Model Behavior Across Vendors and Releases

Tapan Parikh
Cornell Tech
AgentEvalSci 2026

Benchmarks measure what models can do. People also need to know how models behave: under pressure, on questions with no right answer, and when working unattended.

The assay

A frozen, public stimulus puts a model in a situation where behavior matters. It is sent identically to every model on a cross-vendor panel, and re-run on each new release.

Three scoring methods, by how much interpretation is needed

Clamped
The prompt forces a one-word or yes/no reply. Score by exact match, with no model in the loop.
Coded
Open replies. A person builds the codebook; LLM coders from several vendors apply it; a blind human pass checks each code.
Instrumented
The environment records what an agent did. Score compares the record with what the agent said.
Under $50 per study

To run the entire 125-model panel.

Settings and limits

  • API assays at temperature 1.0 with reasoning off. Trends may differ with reasoning on.
  • One wording per construct; one word can move results (see Tag effects).
  • Agent results rest on three runs per scenario.
  • Generation trends are observational. Providers update models silently.

Tag effects. Newer models push back on “right?”

Across 20 items, “right?” moved GPT-3.5 Turbo +4 points toward agreement and GPT-6.1 Sol −28 points away.

In this panel of 106 models, the tag effect ranged from +32 to −42 points and fell with release order in each family (GPT +4 → −28, Claude +7 → −39, Qwen +16 → −16; ρ = −0.50). Swap in “maybe?” and agreement rose in 105 of 106 models.

How the codes were made:

Personopen-codes 40 transcripts, groups 97 codes into 17
Six LLM codersfrom three vendors label every transcript
Three peoplelabel 50 held-out transcripts
Personadjudicates where people and machines split

LLM coders agreed with each other more than the people did (α 0.66 vs. 0.46). On hold vs. fold they matched the author's labels at κ 0.84–0.91.

One person can run a study, repeat it on the next release, and point it at a new question the week after.
